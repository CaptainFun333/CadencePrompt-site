Why I built Cadence Prompt

Eighteen years of live shows, and one tool I got tired of working around.

I’ve worked in live events for eighteen years. I got my start cleaning the office for a neighbor who ran a teleprompter company, and before long I was running prompter, presentation graphics, and media servers on shows of every size.

The software I used most was a great tool once. Over the years, I learned every one of its quirks and bugs by heart. I built workarounds with a Stream Deck and a long list of ways to make things more efficient, most of them using apps outside the prompter. Then one day I caught myself building tools just to make a broken tool behave, and I decided to build the right tool instead.

The very first thing I fixed was coloring text. It used to take four or five clicks just to color a single cue: select the text, right-click, pick color, choose text or background, hit OK. Doing that for an entire script was maddening. Now it takes one click. That’s how Cadence Prompt started: one small, annoying headache, fixed for real.

I named it Cadence for the feel of movement, rhythm, and pace. When a prompter does its job, the presenter stops reading and starts connecting.

What I’m here to do

I want to make live events better for presenters, operators, and crews. Better enough that none of them can imagine running a show without the calm and connection Cadence Prompt brings to the room.

In live events, clients are sometimes under-prepared, and the clock never stops moving. The answer isn’t to stress about it. It’s to be over-prepared enough to handle whatever lands at five o’clock. I wanted a prompter that’s just as good for the crew behind the scenes as it is for the presenter on stage. I’m not trying to build the loudest tool in the room. I want to build the one that shows up on every gig and does exactly what it promised.

What I hold to

  • Accuracy before speed. A late change is only fast if it’s right. Cadence shows you what changed and applies nothing silently.
  • Calm in the moment. The screen should never add to the pressure.
  • Every seat in the room matters. The operator, the presenter, and the crew each get something built for them.
  • Honest by default. If it’s not built, it says coming soon. If it’s a claim, it’s checked.
  • Built from real shows. Every feature starts with a problem someone lived.
  • Solve what you thought you had to live with. Add value without being asked.

What I’ll do for you

Tell me what’s working and what still needs fixing. If something’s broken, I’m ready to make it right. Share your wins too. Hearing what saved you on a real show tells me exactly what to protect.
